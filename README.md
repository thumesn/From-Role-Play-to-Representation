# From Role-Play to Representation: Persona Probes Reveal a Controllable Strategy Subspace in Language Models

## Abstract

Large language models are increasingly used in settings that require strategic judgment, but it remains unclear how such strategic choices are internally organized. Economic games provide a controlled setting for studying this question, yet behavior under standard prompts is often too concentrated to cleanly expose strategy-relevant internal variation. Prior work shows that prompting and persona framing can substantially shift model behavior, but the interpretation of this effect is unresolved: persona may induce only surface-level role-play, or it may expose latent variables that already structure strategic choice. We investigate this question in the Prisoner's Dilemma using contrastive persona prompting as a controlled probe of behavioral divergence under a fixed game specification. This probe reveals a low-dimensional strategy subspace in hidden-state space. Steering along the discovered directions produces graded shifts in cooperative behavior, random direction and random layer controls are substantially weaker, and the same controller remains effective after persona removal. The PD-derived direction also partially transfers to Dictator and Public Goods, indicating that the discovered structure is not reducible to the original persona scaffold or to a single prompt instance. These results support a bounded mechanistic interpretation of persona effects: in strategic settings, persona contrast can serve not only as a behavioral manipulation, but also as a probe for behaviorally relevant internal strategy structure.
